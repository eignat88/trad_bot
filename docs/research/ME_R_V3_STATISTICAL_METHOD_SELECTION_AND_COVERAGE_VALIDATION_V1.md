## Equal-dataset and randomness design

Both methods process the exact same pairs generated by `generate_pairs(scenario, seed=SEED + 7919*k, n=n)`. Envelope bootstrap randomness is independent from data generation (seed `SEED + 15427*k`). Two-way t is deterministic per dataset.

`PILOT_SEED=20261014`; reserved independent `HOLDOUT_SEED=20261119`. Existing code's six scenarios: IID, SYMBOL_SHOCK, DAY_SHOCK, CROSSED_SHOCK, UNBALANCED_CROSSED, HEAVY_TAIL_CROSSED. Sample sizes `n=100` and `n=300`; default generator uses 20 symbols and 25 days. The benchmark has 12 scenario/design cells per candidate.

## Predeclared screening gates (research proposal; NOT yet operator-approved)

**Holdout minimum:** 1,000 generated datasets **per** scenario/design cell, at least 300 envelope resamples per dataset. Run once per frozen candidate implementation. For all 12 cells, both `invalid` and any non-finite outcomes must be accounted for; invalid intervals are failures in unconditional coverage.

A candidate passes this **synthetic screening only** if, for **every** cell:
- `invalid == 0`;
- the two-sided 95% Wilson lower bound of `coverage_all` is at least **0.93** (nominal 0.95 minus 0.02 predefined tolerance).

The Wilson bound makes a threshold at 1,000 simulations stricter than raw 93%; all reported cells include `coverage_all`, `coverage_valid`, invalids, average valid width, simulation uncertainty. Passing does not license use of the candidate for confirmatory inference; failure implies **NO_METHOD_APPROVED**, not a search for a more favorable seed. Even after a screening pass, the output's confirmatory disposition is always `NO_METHOD_APPROVED`.

Important limitations still unresolved: calibration for real-world crossed dependence / leverage, missingness and excluded events, variation in cluster counts, non-positive variance, sparse clusters, optional stopping, resampling mathematics, and multiple-cell familywise error. A mathematical-method review and separate unseen scenario validation are required before statistical approval.
